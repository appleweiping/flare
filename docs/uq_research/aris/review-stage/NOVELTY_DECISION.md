## Proposed contribution and claim audit

The proposed work measures how protecting scarce true transient classes throughout a scheduled light-curve path changes correct commitments, unresolved candidates, emitted-label precision and resource consumption, and tests a small prefix-only continuation ranker at equal archived-query budgets. The calibrated set construction is reused methodology.

| Core claim | Closest work | Independently defensible delta |
|---|---|---|
| Class-conditional trajectory protection permits a first-singleton commitment | Habib & Shihab, [SSCEE, arXiv:2610.20773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20773), C.3/D.1 Proposition 2 | No new generic construction or theorem established; the delta would be the astronomy frontier and its operational consequences under scarce calibration. |
| Valid sets can guide utility-aware continuation | Li et al., [COINS, arXiv:2609.07112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07112); Achenchabe et al., [cost-based early classification, arXiv:2005.09945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09945) | A measurable matched-budget result with fixed astronomical scores and explicit true-class versus predicted-label risks; no claim to invent learning when to wait. |

The exact reason for CAUTION is the absence of a demonstrated mechanism delta over named pathwise/Mondrian and utility-acquisition constructions, combined with prior public studies of the static rare-class failure narrative. This is a concrete thin-delta concern, not a veto based on crowding. The recent exact overlaps are preprints; this review does not promote their venue status or use concurrency to forbid the empirical question.

## Ranking by expected information per available pilot budget

The criterion is how cheaply a candidate resolves the anchored uncertainty, not whether code for it already exists. This is an ordinal allocation decision, not a scored novelty competition. All eight candidates are retained below; deferred means untested, not disproved.

| Rank | Candidate | Decision and reason |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | L1: protection cliff and rank resolution | **Run first.** Counts alone can falsify useful deterministic singleton production at stringent alpha, before any learner is trained. The operational consequence of a permanently retained rare class is testable at negligible cost. Current Block 1 is a reduced L1 screen over declared alphas/deadlines; it does not complete the proposed calibration-size subsampling study. Do not claim that larger study ran. |
| 2 | L3: continuation value under a quota | **Run the small frozen version after feasibility.** This directly tests the remaining actionable uncertainty: whether useful decisions can be redistributed at a common checkpoint budget once sets are fixed. | 3 | L2: explicit loss of abstention and wrong labels | **Use the existing outcome vector as immediate interpretation; defer a claimed scientific cost optimum.** Correct, wrong and unresolved counts plus emitted-label precision have almost no additional compute cost and prevent a misleading success story. A scalar cost matrix requires externally defensible weights; fitting weights after seeing the winner is not acceptable. |
| 4 | L8: COINS survivor-allocation challenger | **Next methodological comparator if a gain survives, not required to finish this diagnostic pilot.** It directly probes whether the max-envelope inefficiency is avoidable with existing stronger methodology. It also requires an explicit class-specific adaptation and tie/tuning audit, beyond the current short execution budget. A general-method claim would need this comparison; the present exploratory implementation does not make that claim. |
| 5 | L6: specialist candidate routing | **Plausible follow-up, not silent replacement of the original endpoint.** It can provide utility when automatic commitments are unsafe, but a class present in every set is not a useful ranking by itself. | 6 | L7: prospective/external feasibility | **Required before external validity or deployment claims, but not a local efficacy experiment.** A future source/label ledger and representative sampling design can identify a real blocker. | 8 | L5: neighbor/support expert | **Defer.** It introduces a new scorer, features and calibration comparison while the present bottleneck may be sample resolution rather than score quality. Sparse rare neighbors and constant-mixture explanations make it a less informative first use of compute. This is not evidence that the idea cannot work. |

## Exact verdict limits applied

=== NOVELTY VERDICT LIMITS (these bound how you judge, never how widely you search) ===
Search exhaustively; judge calibrated. Two failures waste months equally:
passing an idea a published paper already contains, and killing a viable idea
because the territory has neighbors.
1. Proximity is information, not a verdict. Someone working nearby goes in the
   report; it is not by itself a reason to reject.
2. ABANDON has exactly one qualification: a specific published paper already
   contains this result — name that paper. No named paper, no ABANDON.
3. Crowded-but-deltaed is PROCEED: state the delta in one sentence a reviewer
   could verify. Thin or contested delta is PROCEED WITH CAUTION — say what
   would make it carry, not why it should die. CAUTION is not a safe middle:
   if you cannot name the specific thing that makes the delta thin, the
   verdict is PROCEED.
4. Concurrent or competing work is not a veto. That is a race — report it and
   let the user decide whether to run it.
5. A direct attack on a central problem is legitimate novelty when nobody has
   executed it well. "This area is hot" does not mean "this area is taken."
6. This check is an early gate, never the last one — more triage, pilots, or
   external review still stand between any idea and a paper, whatever order
   this run uses. A wrongly passed idea dies cheaply at one of them; a wrongly
   killed idea is never seen again. When torn between two verdicts, choose the
   more permissive one.
Say plainly when an idea clears the check. Do not manufacture overlap.
