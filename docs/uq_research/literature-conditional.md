# Conditional UQ literature audit and independent research design

Prepared 2026-11-02. This is a literature and design artifact, not a result of running candidate-selection experiments. No sealed predictions or test arrays were read. Existing aggregate findings were read from `docs/uq_conditional_calibration.md` and its registered design.

## Recommendation

Study **trajectory-level selective classification at a fixed error risk and review budget**, using FLARE's frozen classifiers and a learned correctness ranking as inputs. Calibrate the complete policy, including its first decision time, on independent object trajectories. This directly targets the service's decision to publish a class, wait for another observation, or defer to review. It avoids treating 90% prediction-set coverage as 90% accuracy among published singleton labels.

This is a research hypothesis and a potentially useful systems contribution, not yet a new statistical theorem. Learn-then-Test already provides the core finite-candidate certification argument. Active anytime-valid risk control already exists in NeurIPS 2024. Innovation would need to lie in a demonstrated astronomy-specific solution to the joint latency, missing-context, rare-class and review-budget problem, supported by deployment-faithful and external evaluation.

## What the existing FLARE study already establishes

The existing branch has already evaluated class-by-energy Mondrian, context and observation partitions, conditional quantile methods, dropout-augmented calibration, weighted covariate-shift correction, APS/RAPS and nonempty-set variants. Energy stratification repairs substantial conditional undercoverage, but grows sets and loses singleton decisions. Its apparent advantage narrows against a size-matched lower-alpha baseline; day-20 efficiency does not improve. It is not a new idea to propose another energy-stratified conformal layer, especially because ASTRANet already does AD-MCP.

The full-curve review trigger decreases the known-class per-analysis review rate from approximately 8% to 1% in development. The reported comparison to random thinning of the old review trigger does not establish optimality against stronger score rankings at the same budget. The 53 anomalies have already been used repeatedly; they are exploratory probes, not independent discovery validation. The public 1,575-object test set has also been inspected. Re-sealing files cannot restore its original confirmatory status.

## Independent design: calibrate the decision trajectory, not each isolated look

### Scientific question and targets

Can a policy using temporal stability and observation quality publish more **correct early classifications** at a fixed certified error risk, while sending no more objects to spectroscopy/review than a confidence-only policy?

One source is the unit of analysis. An object trajectory is `Z_i = (Y_i, X_i,5, X_i,10, X_i,20, X_i,100)`. A policy observes only prefixes available at its current look and chooses `publish(class)`, `wait`, or `defer`. Its first publication time is `tau_pi`. Define `A_pi` as whether it ever publishes by the endpoint and `B_pi = A_pi * 1{published class != Y}`. The main risk is `R(pi) = E[B_pi] / E[A_pi]`, the error among published decisions, with a conservative no-publication fallback when the denominator is zero.

The full policy includes its stopping rule. Calibrating complete trajectories controls errors at the policy's selected time under object-level assumptions; it does not rely on four prefix records being independent. This is different from taking four separately calibrated confidence statements and selecting whichever looks best after observation.

### Candidate policy and leakage controls

Use the shipped hierarchy label as the classification rule. Learn a small correctness ranking on **independent policy-training objects**, with candidate inputs: class probabilities and margins, fold disagreement, energy rank within the same model, context-presence bits, number/cadence/SNR of observations, and change in prediction or uncertainty since prior looks. All features must use the observed prefix. A model-specific rank avoids silently treating energy scales from separate LightGBM fits as interchangeable.

The meta-model estimates correctness or the benefit of waiting; it does not invent a seventh class from low confidence. Benchmark novelty detection is a separate task. Deferral labels and scientific utility need to be defined before scoring. Waiting for the next recorded look can be replayed offline; claiming optimal telescope acquisition cannot, because the actual observation schedule was not randomized and its counterfactual outcomes are absent.

Use a strict split for each evaluated policy: base-model training, policy learning, certification, final development evaluation. Alternatively use an outer evaluation loop containing these roles, but never mix fold/refit predictions when calibrating. Every certificate is for a fixed deployed ensemble/policy and a stated target population. Pooling overlapping cross-fit predictions does not automatically produce an i.i.d. binomial certificate for a newly refitted final model.

### A simple valid certificate before any sophisticated extension

Freeze a finite grid of `L` policies before opening the certification split. For each policy, let `m` be the number of accepted certification objects and `k` the number of erroneous accepted labels. Under i.i.d. object sampling and a policy independent of certification labels, conditionally on `m`, `k` is binomial with parameter equal to population selective risk. For the unsafe null `R(pi) >= epsilon`, a valid one-sided p-value is `BinomCDF(k; m, epsilon)`; use p=1 when m=0.

Use Bonferroni at `delta/L` or a justified, preordered Learn-then-Test procedure. Among certified policies choose the highest independently estimated utility/lowest delay. More general bounded scientific losses can use LTT's bounded-loss tests. A high-confidence statement about budget or acceptance also needs its own valid bound; matching empirical average budget alone is an empirical comparison.

For predetermined groups, define the same accepted-error risk within each group. To demand that a candidate satisfy every group requirement, its composite unsafe null is a union; the maximum of the group p-values is valid for that null, as in LTT Proposition 6. Do not present uncertifiable groups as certified. It is also possible to certify a pooled/superclass policy and report finer groups descriptively, provided the change of claim is explicit.

### Rare-class power limit

Even with zero observed accepted errors, a one-sided Clopper–Pearson upper bound is `1 - delta_eff^(1/m)`. At 95% confidence, certifying risk <=5% requires at least 59 accepted examples; risk <=1% requires 299. With 20 Bonferroni-corrected candidate policies these optimistic floors become 117 and 597. Real errors increase the requirement. TDE has only 68 objects in the entire benchmark, so honest class-specific certification at these levels is usually impossible after splitting. No estimator or pooling trick should conceal that limitation. This is a reason to specify useful superclass and pooled guarantees, not to claim rare-class safety without data.

### Missing-context and distribution-shift claim

Use the existing 16 context masks and sparse-photometry transformations as prespecified stress interventions, with paired source-level evaluation. All views of a source must stay in the same split. Certifying each fixed transform can be done with multiple-risk testing across transforms while preserving object-level samples. It does **not** guarantee performance for arbitrary feature-dependent or label-dependent mixtures of interventions. A max-loss over all allowed views can support a stronger robust *joint-error* target, but selective-risk control additionally requires a nontrivial acceptance denominator; it may be vacuous.

True Fritz deployment validation needs representative labeled target trajectories or a stated, justified shift model. Current synthetic masking/thinning is a robustness experiment, not an external survey validation. The public historical test set remains descriptive.

### Frozen baselines and falsifiers

Evaluate all methods with the same base models, training data and certification split:

1. Maximum probability / margin threshold, chosen with the same LTT certificate.
2. The current FLARE first-singleton publication rule, plus a stricter-alpha variant matched for publication rate or review budget.
3. Energy/consensus-only ranking with the same certificate.
4. Temporal-stability-only ranking, without the learned meta-model.
5. The full learned ranking; ablate context/quality signals and history separately.
6. A fixed-day policy at days 5/10/20, exposing the latency–accuracy tradeoff.
7. If available, the proposed fold-wise e-value/anytime policy with the same endpoint and budget; do not compare anomaly FDR with classification error as if they were identical risks.

Report certified error target and confidence, actual selective error with intervals, publication fraction, time to first correct publication, review fraction, each class's recall among published labels, group coverage, calibration/model-fit variation, and compute/storage cost. Use source-level paired bootstrap for *empirical differences* and repeat the entire calibration fit across prespecified splits for calibration variability. Bootstrap CIs are not the finite-sample risk certificate.

Predeclare a small grid, primary day-20 utility, primary pooled selective-risk target (e.g. 5% with delta=0.05), meaningful gain at equal budget, and group guardrails **before scoring**. Reject the proposed learned policy if it cannot outperform max-probability plus the same certificate, if gains disappear after equal-budget matching, if it only improves common SN Ia at the expense of rare classes, or if certificates become vacuous. A negative result would still establish whether FLARE's current scores already extract the relevant ordering information.

## What would make this publishable, and what cannot be delivered by local code alone

A credible paper needs a reproducible object-level decision benchmark, genuinely useful utility–risk–latency gains beyond strong matched baselines, a precise proof of whichever guarantee is claimed, and external/prospective validation with labels. Merely adding LTT, e-values, conformal sets or a meta-model to FLARE is not by itself a new top-venue methodological contribution. The strongest possible astronomy contribution may be an honest deployment study quantifying operational gains under data-quality variation; the appropriate venue depends on the results.

An active-spectroscopy extension should log random query propensities and preserve exploration support, borrowing the active-RCPS literature rather than pretending that selectively obtained spectra form an unbiased calibration sample. That requires collaboration with the service maintainers and a prospective data protocol. It is a later stage, not a result obtainable from the existing 53 anomalies.
