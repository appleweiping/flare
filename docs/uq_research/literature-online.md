# Online and lifetime uncertainty: verified literature and FLARE research design

Prepared 2026-11-02. This is a source-checked research memo, not an experimental result. No sealed predictions or test arrays were inspected. Existing branch documents and `flare/lifetime.py` were read. Paper landing pages and PDFs are retained in `literature-online/` beside this memo; text was extracted with `pdftotext`.

## Main conclusion

There are two different sequential axes in FLARE: repeated visits to the **same source** while its light curve grows, and **different sources arriving through calendar time** under changing selection/cadence. Online adaptive conformal methods principally address the latter when outcome feedback arrives. They do not automatically solve the former's repeated-look error. ACI cannot be installed by treating every light-curve visit as another independent, promptly labelled source.

For immediate work, an honest split trajectory-max calibration is a useful, interpretable lifetime-alarm baseline and a statistical correction to the deployed replay heuristic. It is not a new generic conformal method: an October 2026 paper gives exactly this max-versus-Bonferroni construction in another domain. A stronger research question is whether a frozen policy using quality/context and uncertainty trajectories improves **correct early decisions and anomaly yield per follow-up resource** under explicit source-level risk constraints.

## Three error rates that must stay distinct

1. **Per-analysis false alarm**: for one fixed look h of a null source, `Pr(A_i,h = 1) <= alpha`. Four looks can raise the chance of any alarm; fifty visits can raise it further. A 1% per-look claim is not 1% lifetime.
2. **Per-source lifetime false alarm**: `Pr(exists h <= H: A_i,h = 1 | known-source population) <= alpha`. Controls one source over a specified schedule/horizon, including stopping on its own accumulated evidence. It does not constrain the fraction of reviewed sources that are false positives; that depends on prevalence and power.
3. **FDR over selected sources**: `E[V / max(R,1)] <= q`. A source must be counted once. Re-running batch BH/e-BH each night and accumulating discoveries is not automatically online FDR control. Known-class FAR times source-entry rate estimates false alarms/day only if the chosen null population and operational rate match deployment; its units and uncertainty differ from FDR.

For class prediction there is a fourth distinction: unconditional `Pr(wrong and accepted) <= alpha` does not imply `Pr(wrong | accepted) <= alpha`. At 10% acceptance, even a 1% unconditional error rate permits 10% selective error.

## Existing FLARE overlap and a concrete gap

`flare/lifetime.py` already calibrates running minima of consensus p-values at each replay index, then takes the minimum of those lifetime p-values and empirically calibrates this whole-life statistic. Its docstring correctly states the minimum is not itself an anytime-valid p-value. The replay schedule is nested: every detection count 3 through 40, then each fifth detection. The original per-analysis 1% alarm can hit about 15% of test objects at some time, according to the docstring.

The existing branch's UQ study reports an improved full-curve review trigger, conditional undercoverage, and fold-versus-refit score mismatch. It already tried group/Mondrian/APS/RAPS/weighted-conformal repairs and measured efficiency failures. Simply rerunning those candidates or relabelling empirical lifetime calibration as an e-process is insufficient.

A real gap remains: source-level guarantees using a **disjoint calibration pool**, a fixed same-model score at calibration and deployment, and operational utility measured at matched lifetime error/inspection budget. The scientific value may be an honest and reproducible deployment correction plus a better policy, even when the constituent statistics are established.

## Proposed independent design: risk-calibrated early decision policy

### Stage 1: exact, modest baseline

Keep the four scientific looks `{5,10,20,100}` days fixed. Fit model and all energy normalizers only on proper training data. For each calibration source form a complete trajectory `Z_i=(X_i,5,...,X_i,100,Y_i)` under a documented observation rule. Let `M_i=max_h g_h(X_i,h)` for a frozen scalar anomaly score. A conservative rank against null calibration maxima applied to the running test maximum controls ever-alarm probability under null-trajectory exchangeability. Training-only per-horizon scaling prevents arbitrary model energy offsets from dominating the maximum.

This is a baseline, not new mathematics. It must be compared to the current lifetime implementation and to both naive repeated thresholds and equal-spending Bonferroni; e-budget comparisons should use a fixed calibrator selected without looking at the evaluation outcomes. Four scheduled looks must not be described as every-detection anytime deployment. Missing/ineligible looks need a fixed conservative convention or an explicit eligible-population target; dropping difficult short curves post hoc changes the population.

### Stage 2: useful policy contribution

Train a small prefix-only quality/policy model on proper training objects. Inputs can include disagreement across independently trained models, missing context, counts and photometric errors, evolution in ambiguity across observed looks, and expected information gain from a next observation. Choose among **wait**, **emit a class**, and **request anomaly review**. Calibrate and compare complete frozen policies at the source level, using disjoint calibration objects; all trajectory views of one source remain in the same partition.

Two principled paths:

- **Simultaneous class sets**: calibrate a class-conditional full-path nonconformity maximum, e.g. `max_h a_h(1-P_h(c))`, where normalizers are fitted before calibration. The running intersection of allowed class sets then supports stopping at the first singleton with unconditional wrong-stop probability controlled by simultaneous coverage. This does not solve selected-sample error; report acceptance and conditional error explicitly.
- **Selective policy calibration**: calibrate a finite, predeclared family of policy thresholds using a risk-control/multiple-testing procedure with a simultaneous bound on selective error, not a naive mean error. If the guarantee involves `E[wrong * accepted] - r E[accepted]`, explain exactly which target is certified, and avoid swapping a ratio of expectations for expected per-batch FDP. Very few accepted rare-class calibration objects can make certification impossible. The correct result may be abstention.

This second stage maps uncertainty-aware recommendation/reranking expertise into resource-allocation utility. It has a stronger scientific question than a new named combination of conformal tools, but novelty still needs direct comparison to established selective prediction, Learn-then-Test, and early-classification research.

### Predeclared evaluation

- Use source-level outer folds; each outer-training pool splits into proper train, policy-selection and **untouched calibration** subsets. Scorers, normalizers, policy family, schedule, and anomaly definitions freeze before outer evaluation. Five outer folds quantify development stability; this remains exploratory because the BTS dataset and old test have been inspected repeatedly.
- Primary outcomes: lifetime known-source false alarms with binomial/object-level intervals; anomaly detections at a fixed lifetime FAR; earliest alert day with nondetections treated as censored/not assigned an invented day; wrong early classifications; accepted fraction; number of requests per source and compute cost. The 53 anomaly objects are a descriptive challenge set until a fresh anomaly cohort is obtained; don’t optimize a policy on them and later call recall confirmatory.
- Fairness controls: matched lifetime FAR **and** matched review budget; same data, model predictions and score directions; final-look-only rule, current deployed empirical lifetime, naive per-look, Bonferroni, fixed e-mixture, full-trajectory max, and no-quality-feature ablations. Report whole curves and the frozen operational point, not the best point found after seeing results.
- Stress tests: prefix-causal thinning, context dropout and cadence changes, keeping all variations of a source in one fold. Label all such tests simulated shifts; no arbitrary-shift finite-sample guarantee survives without corresponding assumptions. The null mix and observation process need matching between calibration and deployment.
- Account for policy fitting and calibration uncertainty: object bootstrap conditional on a single fitted policy is not the full algorithm's sampling uncertainty. Use outer-fold variability and, where affordable, repeated complete train/calibration splits; do not bootstrap individual visits.

### External phase required for a publication claim

Freeze artifact hashes, source IDs, training dates, score recipe, calibration pool, policy and operating targets before receiving an untouched calendar cohort. On Fritz/SkyPortal replay, record actual analysis times, first detection, trigger eligibility, context as it was available, follow-up requests, and eventual label/revelation time. Retrospective final labels must not be made available at earlier steps. Separate a representative known-source calibration sample from actively selected unusual sources; spectroscopy missingness must be recorded, not ignored. If a representative label sample cannot be obtained, claims apply only to the labelled selection population.

An ACI/DtACI/decaying-step extension belongs here, once label delays and selection are measurable. Until then it can be simulated as a sensitivity study but not asserted to be deployment-valid. Neither the existing archive nor the quoted conversation establishes that a fresh external cohort is available.
